## 4. Limitations (honest, per card)

- Rate fidelity: no spikes, no per-cell kinetics; the claim is about the
  MODEL class, stated as such — not about the fly's motor system.
- Frequency claims are BAND-RELATIVE, and the sub-band line is
  STIMULUS-SET (§2.8): the drive envelope's own dominant spectral line
  is .100 Hz [l171.envelope], so the census numbers below describe
  where a stimulus-locked line sits, not an endogenous clock. The
  registered dominant-frequency read lives in the 0.3–10 Hz lock band,
  while the substrate's global
  spectral peak often sits BELOW it (16/16 on-drive vs 11/16 mech-off
  seeds; untruncated mean .10 → .20 Hz) [l154.band]. A census of every
  stored lineage trace quantifies the pattern: in the l65-baseline arm
  the global peak is sub-band in 449 of 449 UNIQUE SEEDS (Wilson lb
  .992) at a stable median .100 Hz that survives linear detrend in every
  case — a real slow oscillation rather than trend wander, but a
  stimulus-locked one (§2.8) — while the intact closure is sub-band in
  348 of 433 unique seeds (lb .764) with a less frequency-stable slow
  component [l172.census_unique]. The trace-level forms of these two
  censuses (518 of 518 and 361 of 500 [l156.pooled]) count the
  forward-chain anchor seeds once per batch; de-duplicating to unique
  seeds removes 69 and 67 repeat observations respectively, with zero
  disagreement between any repeat pair, and moves the closure rate from
  .722 to .804 [l172.census_unique]. A per-pool carrier census
  (two fresh batches, B33/B34) shows this line is not
  antagonist-clock-wide: the mm/xm aggregates carry it in 36 of 36
  traces each, and a Tr/Ta/Sr subset of the antagonist pools in 8 of 20
  (pooled Wilson lb ≥ .56, subset median .100 Hz; strongest members 36/36),
  while the Ti pools stay at ≤ 6 of 36 and the strongest Pr pool reaches
  20 of 36 (lb .396) [l157.pooled] (Fig. 3). The post-hoc untruncated
  sign read (11+/5−) is labeled descriptive, not claim-carrying.
- Readout is simulated motor-pool structure duty, not behavior or EMG.
- Single specimen per connectome (BANC v626 one female; MaleCNS one male);
  no replication across specimens exists yet at single-cell resolution.
  Our cross-connectome comparisons also join cell types by type NAME, a
  string-level operation whose conventions differ between specimens — our
  own cross-graph port found the winning sign arm flipping between the
  male and the female substrate. Connectome alignment by network topology
  alone now assigns types to about 13,000 neurons intrinsic to the female
  ventral nerve cord from two male references [37], which is the
  principled replacement for that string join; until §2.6's scope arm is
  redone that way it should be read as string-joined.
- OPERATING-POINT PROXIMITY, with a convergent disclosure from the
  nearest prior art: a 15% synaptic-amplitude reduction abolished intact
  wing-motor output in the steering model of [36], whose authors state
  that their lesion collapse may therefore partly reflect proximity to
  threshold. The same failure mode applies to any rate or spiking model
  pinned near a boundary, including ours. Our instrument for it is the
  five-set af-matched gain ensemble (§2.5), which re-bisects the gain per
  jitter set and reports the cliff endpoints separately from the
  transition interior; we claim that the fragility is measured here, not
  that it is absent.
- The transition interior is gain-regime-dependent (2.5) — we report the
  boundary rather than a universal curve.
- UNIT OF INFERENCE: every interval in this paper is over NOISE SEEDS of
  ONE FIXED NETWORK — one graph, one bisected gain, one drive protocol;
  the seed sets the noise stream and the initial state, nothing else.
  They are therefore not statements about variability across connectomes,
  specimens, extraction cohorts or gains. The only places where the
  network itself varies are the five-set gain ensemble (§2.5), the
  cross-connectome scope arm (§2.6) and the rewire draws (§2.7), each
  reported separately and never pooled with the seed intervals. The same
  discipline is applied independently in [36], where the unit of inference
  is the simulation realization rather than its twenty gust trajectories.
- Arm depth is unequal by construction: an arm's canonical count unions
  every admissible batch that ran it, so the dose-curve arms reach 178–258
  seeds while the mechanism arms reach 34–98 (eOutMot is the thinnest at
  34, and the motor+in combo remains SINGLE-BATCH at n=16) [l172.pooled].
  Comparisons between arms of very different depth are reported as
  differences with intervals rather than as class labels.
- DATA FLOOR: the BANC v626 connections release carries an IMPLICIT
  pair-level synapse floor of 3 — all 2,676,592 pairs sum ≥ 3 (811,821
  exactly at 3, zero below; the 574,733 one-synapse per-neuropil ROWS
  exist only as intra-pair breakdowns: 462,170 pairs contain such a row
  yet still sum ≥ 3) [l146.release] — so a single-synapse sensitivity
  critique has no purchase on this release's pair weights. The
  Kopel-class caveat that does apply is about DETECTION rather than
  weight: in that preregistered screen, two of five criteria fail on
  BANC at a 2–3-synapse floor while the male and FlyWire volumes hold to
  thresholds of 10–15, and the stated cause is measurement — only 22% of
  BANC's detected synapses end on an identified neuron, against 41.9%
  and 40.1% in the other two volumes [12]. Our floor arms bound weight
  sensitivity; they cannot bound attachment completeness, and no result
  here should be read as doing so. Under the
  strongest ADMISSIBLE floor (f4: keep pairs with ≥ 4 synapses, deleting
  27.1% of closure edges, 647,953 → 472,095 [l146.f4]) the CARRIER is
  robust — rank 25 → 21 (inside the 19–29 window), 69/80 edges survive,
  heavy-cohort Jaccard .9231 — while the TWIN selection is
  floor-3-contingent (twin rank 26 → 33, adjacency does not port): the
  identity claim and the twin-XOR selection have different data
  dependencies [l146.f4]. The floor read is fully RE-DERIVED, not
  frozen: K (77 → 73), heavy-cohort membership (five cells out, one in)
  and the mass ordering are all recomputed under f4 [l169b.forms].
  Freezing the cohort SET and re-ordering alone already reproduces both
  ranks (21 and 33), so the carrier's shift is re-ordering rather than
  turnover [l169b.forms]. The f4 bound is STATIC ONLY: the dynamic f4
  arm has now been run (fresh per-arm af-matched bisects on the
  floor-4 graph, cut at the re-derived window — f4wA = first 20, f4ng =
  first 21 including the carrier, f4tw = first 20 plus the f4 mass-twin
  DNpe005-R; one batch, n = 16) and the registered R-side cliff does NOT
  transfer: all three f4 arms sit at kR 0/16 (meanR .043/.081/.082,
  upper bound .194) while the base-substrate arms reproduce on the SAME
  seeds (cutng25 SUPER 16/16 meanR .754, cutwA PARTIAL 3/16 .371), and
  the f4ng-vs-f4tw XOR leg is flat (−.001 paired, 8 up / 8 down)
  [l169b2.dyn]. Registered branch G2_no_transfer: the floor-4 robustness
  of the identity claim is a property of the STATIC ladder, not of the
  dynamics on the floored substrate — consistent with our
  substrate-rule law that structure results do not port between
  extraction rules. One post-hoc observation is reported without a
  claim: on the floored substrate the structure moves to the LEFT side
  (f4wA meanL .698, kL 16/16, versus .233 for base cutwA), where carrier
  removal COSTS structure (f4ng .289, paired −.409, 16/16 seeds down)
  more than its mass-matched twin does (f4tw .560, paired −.139)
  [l169b2.lat_descriptive]; a registered left-side test is queued and
  nothing in §2 depends on it.
- Wet-lab predictions are NOT derived here.
