## 5. Discussion

A connectome-constrained model is usually asked whether wiring matters. We asked it which cell matters, with the comparison currency fixed in advance. Removing descending-neuron cohorts of increasing size from a bulk-extracted cord subnet produces no dose curve but a non-monotone, all-or-none window (§2.1), and the step between the two sides of that window is carried by one cell's identity rather than by the mass its removal takes out of the graph (§2.2). The mass-twin control is what licenses that sentence: a second cell whose removal mass differs from the carrier's in the fourth decimal lands intermediate rather than neutral, so the step cannot be read as a mass threshold that the larger cut happens to cross.

The carrier is a sufficient route into the super class rather than the only one, and its sufficiency is set-dependent rather than mass-dependent. Of seven carrier-free cohorts matched to the same removal-mass window, one reaches the super class (39 of 48) and six do not, and ordering the seven by mass does not order them by effect (§2.2). The one that reaches it raises both sides instead of reproducing the carrier's crossed signature, so it arrives in the same class through a different state. We therefore state the finding as sufficiency under a named cohort and a named window, and the title carries that scope.

What the carrier contributes is better described as a channel than as a firing pattern (§2.4). Its incident edge set reproduces the flip and either subset of that edge set suffices, while clamping the cell's own activity completes the flip on the cut base (78 of 98) and does not detectably move the intact subnet. A cell can therefore be load-bearing in a model without its own activity being the variable that carries the effect. Neither a removal experiment nor a silencing experiment can draw that distinction alone; the two have to be run on the same substrate at a matched operating point, which is what the gain bisect in §3.4 is for.

Single-cell necessity in a connectome-constrained model is established prior art (§1(a)), and so is identity beating cell number in a fly connectome model (§1(d)). Our contribution sits in the controls rather than in the headline. The step emerges in an uncurated, bulk-extracted subnet rather than in a hand-curated circuit, and it survives a control matched on removal mass rather than on cell count. Those are different currencies, and the difference is large here: the carrier's own removal mass sits 9.72× below any band in which mass alone reproduces the step (§2.2). Where the nearest prior result grafts cells back by type, our unit is one cell against one mass twin.

The robustness picture is deliberately split rather than summarised in one word. Under a five-set gain-jitter ensemble the endpoints of the transition hold (5 of 5) while its interior re-draws (0 of 5), and the interior is exactly where a reader would otherwise over-read the law (§2.5). The same discipline applies across substrates: the ladder does not transfer to the male connectome at a matched synapse floor (§2.6), and on the floored female substrate the static robustness of the identity claim does not carry into the dynamics (§4). A result measured on an extracted subnet is scoped to the extraction rule that produced it. This is the instability that the field's own stress-testing of connectome-constrained models reports (§1(c)), and our answer to it is not immunity but that every arm, band and branch was registered before the numbers existed (§3.8).

One claim in this lineage was withdrawn by its own controls, and we keep it in view rather than deleting it (§2.8). A sub-band spectral line we had read as a property of the substrate sits where the drive envelope's own spectrum peaks, so the line's position is stimulus-set and needs no endogenous antagonist clock. The identity contrast does not ride on it: re-scored in the unchopped segment and after a high-pass, the carrier-versus-twin ordering is preserved. The transferable rule is that a frequency claim in a driven model stays band-relative until the drive's own spectrum has been computed on the same window, and we hold our own earlier reading to it.

None of this is a statement about the animal. The substrate is a rate model on a static wiring diagram, without neuron-level kinetics, neuromodulation or a body, the readout is an internal structure statistic rather than a behaviour, and §4 enumerates what follows from that rather than this section repeating it. What the result does buy is a falsifiable target with a named cell, a named window and a named currency: on this substrate, removing or silencing that cell should cross the structure statistic while its mass twin should not, and a spiking or wet-lab replication can refute that without reproducing our pipeline. The carrier's homolog in the female brain connectome is identified in the data note, which is where a live-brain version of the same question would start.
