### 2.7 Pre-posting controls: the gain swaps, the rewire and the deletion (L169 stages a–d)

Every arm is re-gained to a matched active fraction, and the flip arms sit
+1.06–1.30% above `g_cutwA` while the non-flip arms sit at ±0.03%. So carrier
removal and a slightly higher gain are confounded along the diagonal of the
experiment above. The control is the off-diagonal: run each graph at the
OTHER cell's bisected gain, on one fresh seed batch (n=16), paired noise.

The 2x2 on Ti right-side duty divides along both axes [l169.grid]. Graph-wA at
its own gain is PARTIAL (meanR .426, kR 4/16); graph-wA at the HIGHER cutng25
gain (`wAgN`) does NOT flip (meanR .317, kR 2/16); graph-cutng25 at the LOWER
cutwA gain (`n25gW`) stays SUPER (meanR .702, kR 15/16, [.717,.989]);
graph-cutng25 at its own gain SUPER (meanR .753, kR 16/16).

Paired on the same batch [l169.swap_legs], `n25gW` sits +.276 above cutwA
(10/16 seeds up) while `wAgN` sits −.109 BELOW it (2/16 up). The whole
confounded gain step, applied to the carrier-intact graph, moves the readout
in the opposite direction to the confound hypothesis.
Registered branch D1 fired [l169.branch]: the transition
follows the GRAPH, not the gain. The gain difference is a real asymmetry in
the design, but it is not the mechanism.

The probe and its anchors are reported next. The swap arms are deliberately
off their matched operating point, which is the test; the af offsets are
±.0010–.0013 [l169.af]. The registered per-seed direction (wAgN above target,
n25gW below) holds in the batch means but only on 15 of 18 seeds — the
per-seed af spread is the size of the 1.24% gain step, and we report that
rather than the mean alone. A swap arm bit-identical to its own-gain twin
would have voided the run; the separation is 1.9e-2 at minimum [l169.nt].

The batch unions were re-pooled with B35 [l169.pooled]:
silW 42/48 (lb .753, three batches), cutng25 140/144 (lb .931, nine),
cutwA 47/160 (ub .368, ten), eMotTop5 19/48, eMotBot5 13/48. These supersede
the two-batch pooled figures quoted in §2.4; one pooled count per arm is the
L172 statistics pass, not this subsection.

Scope: one fresh batch per new cell, and a two-point gain probe cannot
locate a threshold between .143658 and .145445. The floor-4 pre-posting
control is reported in §4 (static bound plus a dynamic arm that does not
transfer). Of the remaining controls, the carrier-edge rewire and the
same-footprint different-class deletion are measured below, and the
carrier-vs-twin pair at swapped gains closes the section.

Design pins for the rewire and deletion arms are stored [l169c.design]. The
contralateral DNp33 cell (gi 928) is a footprint mirror of the carrier but
not a cohort mirror. It carries 88 closure out-edges against the carrier's
80, and out-mass 200.05 against 183.96, with near-identical tier splits.
It also receives cord input, so the pure-projector extraction rule never
admits it to the ranked cohort and no cut window contains it. Its target
cells are almost disjoint from the carrier's sheet (6 shared, Jaccard
.037), while its direct motor edges mirror the side (10 right / 1 left
against the carrier's 9 left / 1 right). We therefore label that arm a
same-footprint, different-class deletion, not a homolog control. The
degree-preserving rewire is admissible here: the partner pool is 804x the
carrier's out-degree, the median draw relocates all 80 edges, and it
returns no measurable mass to the carrier's own target sheet.

**Displacement is not removal, and the class axis is specific (stage c2).**
Both arms were then run on a fresh batch (B37, n=16 each, paired noise,
fresh af-matched bisect per arm). The base arms reproduce on the same seeds
— cutwA PARTIAL (meanR .452, kR 5/16) and cutng25 SUPER (.786, 15/16) — so
the comparison is not vacuous [l169c2.base].

On the rewire arm [l169c2.rew], three independent draws of the registered
degree-preserving rewire SPLIT. One flips (`rew1` meanR .724, kR 16/16,
paired +.272 over cutwA, 15/16 seeds up). Two do not (`rew2` .333, 1/16,
paired −.119; `rew3` .302, 3/16, paired −.150). Both non-flip draws sit BELOW
the carrier-intact base, with 15/16 and 12/16 seeds down. The draw pool is
20 of 48 [.288,.557], straddling the same-batch cutwA interval [.142,.556] and
far below cutng25's [.717,.989]. The registered reading [l169c2.branch] is
that the 2-of-3 majority fires R2_mass_degree — displacing the carrier's mass
is not equivalent to removing it. The pooled-straddle
clause of R3_draw_lottery is also true, so we report the conjunction. A
degree-preserving rewire is bracketed by the carrier-intact base rather than
by carrier removal, and which way one draw lands is a lottery. Mis-aiming the
same mass costs structure relative to aiming it correctly; it does not buy the
flip.

This is not a gain artifact [l169c2.gains]: the three draws' re-bisected gains
are not ordered with their readouts. `rew3` carries the smallest gain step
over g_cutwA (.144464) and reads .302; `rew1` the middle one
(.145617) and reads .724; `rew2` the largest (.150060) and reads .333. The
readout is non-monotone in gain, which is what the graph-tracking
verdict of the 2x2 above predicts.

On the deletion arm [l169c2.hom], deleting gi 928 — same footprint, different
subnet class, 101 incident closure edges removed — does NOT flip the readout
(meanR .301, kR 2/16, [.035,.360], paired −.150, 13/16 seeds down). Registered
branch H1_specific fires. With the mass-matched twin and the carrier-free
scramble cohorts of §2.2, the flip is not bought by matched mass, nor by
matched footprint in a different class, nor reliably by displacing the
carrier's own edges.

The anchors hold [l169c2.cert]: all twelve inherited arms are bit-identical to
the prior run at the anchor seeds (max 0.0), the four stored-form bisects
re-land exactly, the in-run rewire draws reproduce the stored design artifact
field-for-field, and every new arm separates from the base in its motor trace
(min 1.9e-2). Coincidence disclosed: `rew3` and `hom` have nearly equal batch
means (.302 / .301) but differ per seed by up to .46 — different arms, not a
duplicated one.

**The XOR pair at swapped gains: identity wins in both gain columns
(stage d).** The 2x2 of §2.7's opening paragraph was run on the cutwA /
cutng25 contrast. The claim the title makes, however, rides the twin-XOR
pair — cutng25 (carrier gi 1110 removed) against cutng25p (the same window
with the mass-twin gi 319 removed instead, a 3.9e-04 mass delta) — and
those two arms sit at bisected gains 1.34% apart, with the twin arm at the
LOWER gain [l169d.grid]. We therefore ran the fully crossed pair on a
further fresh batch (B38, n=16, paired noise): each graph at its own
af-matched gain and at the other arm's.

The own-gain diagonal reproduces on B38 [l169d.grid]: cutng25 SUPER
(meanR .798, kR 16/16, [.806,1.000]) and cutng25p NOT-SUPER
(.477, kR 8/16, [.280,.720]), paired +.321 with 16 of 16 seeds up
[l169d.legs]. The registered batch prediction holds: against the stored
earlier batch the cells move only in level, not in class (cutng25
15/16 → 16/16, cutng25p 5/16 → 8/16) [l169d.xbatch]. Off the diagonal
[l169d.grid], the twin graph run at the carrier arm's HIGHER gain (`p25gN`)
does not flip — meanR .324, kR 1/16, paired −.152 below its own-gain self
(12/16 seeds down). The carrier graph run at the twin arm's LOWER gain
(`n25gP`) stays SUPER — meanR .651, kR 15/16, [.717,.989], paired −.147 but
still above the SUPER bar.

Read at FIXED gain, the contrast is maximal [l169d.legs]: at g_cutng25 the
carrier-removed graph is
+.474 above the twin-removed graph with 16 of 16 seeds up, and
at g_cutng25p it is +.174 with 12 of 16 up. Neither gain column reverses the
ordering; both columns put the carrier-removed graph on top. Registered branch
E1_graph_tracking fired [l169d.branch], the strongest of the four branches:
the twin arm does not reach SUPER when given the carrier arm's gain, and the
carrier arm does not fall out of SUPER when given the twin arm's. Cell
identity, not the matched-gain difference, carries the transition on BOTH
sides of the pair.

The boundaries are reported honestly. The twin arm sits AT the NOT-SUPER
boundary on this batch (kR exactly 8 of 16, meanR .477 just under the .50 bar)
— it is the weakest cell in the grid and is reported as a boundary read, not a
clean negative. Raising a gain did not help either graph (both swap arms read
below their own-gain selves), so the two-point probe bounds the readout at
.143527 and .145445 and still cannot locate a threshold between them. The
registered per-seed af direction (p25gN above target, n25gP below)
holds on 15 and 13 of 18 seeds respectively and is reported PARTIAL
[l169d.cert].

The anchors hold [l169d.cert]. All sixteen inherited arms are bit-identical to
the prior run at the anchor seeds (max 0.0), and the twin arm is additionally
bit-identical to the much earlier run that first rolled it (max 0.0), which
certifies the new arm's wiring for free. Its fresh bisect re-lands the stored
gain exactly; staircase residual 5.4e-07; each swap arm separates from its
own-gain self in the motor trace (min 1.9e-2).

With stage (d) the four pre-posting controls of this section are complete:
gain swap on the cutwA contrast (a), synapse-floor transfer (b, §4),
edge rewire and same-footprint different-class deletion (c), and the XOR
pair at swapped gains (d).
