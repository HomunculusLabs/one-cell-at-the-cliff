Connectome-constrained simulations invite single-cell causal claims, but such claims can depend on model parameters, intervention type and how the circuit was extracted. We simulate a 21,809-neuron nerve-cord subnetwork of the female Drosophila BANC connectome as a rate model and delete descending neurons (DNs) in order of projection mass, re-matching each lesioned network to a common active fraction. An antiphase readout of the right tibia flexor and extensor motor pools, present in 38% of noise seeds in the unlesioned model (66/176), is abolished after deleting the 23 heaviest non-glutamatergic DNs of the cohort (0/64), partly returns at 24 (43/144) and becomes near-certain at 25 (124/128). The 24-to-25 step is one cell, DNp33-L; deleting a mass-matched cell instead (DNpe025-R) leaves the readout near baseline (12/32), so removal mass does not determine the outcome. The effect is context-dependent: silencing DNp33-L does not raise the readout in the unlesioned model but flips the 24-cell-lesioned model at unchanged gain (28/32 across two seed batches), as does deleting its 70 outputs onto interneurons or its 13 inputs. Under 1% weight jitter with re-matched gain the 25-cell lesion keeps its outcome in five of five networks, but the 24-cell baseline does not, and the carrier-versus-control contrast is clear in three. A lesion set that spares DNp33-L reaches a similar state, so the cell is sufficient on this base rather than necessary. The ladder is defined by an extraction rule and a three-synapse floor, and transfer to a second connectome is untested. We report an internally preregistered, seed-replicated case of an epistatic single-neuron effect in a connectome model: a statement about this model class, not about fly behaviour.
